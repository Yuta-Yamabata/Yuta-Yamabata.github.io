Abstract

Creating expressive motions for dynamic legged robots often requires labor-intensive, robot-specific motion design that scales poorly. We propose an end-to-end pipeline that retargets emotion-labeled human gait to a low-cost duck-like bipedal robot while preserving transferable affective cues: head orientation, forward speed, and gait period. Using these cues, the pipeline generates reference motions with a walking pattern generator and inverse kinematics, trains reinforcement learning policies to imitate them, and executes the resulting motions stably on hardware. The resulting robot motions preserved the ordering across emotion conditions observed in the human data for gait period and forward speed and realized a distinct pitch cue for sadness. A video-based user study reveals selective preservation of affective legibility through retargeting. Sadness was robustly preserved and neutral was partially preserved, whereas anger and happiness shifted toward different perceptual neighbors. These patterns may reflect source ambiguity, omitted affective cues, hardware limits, and the robot’s non-threatening appearance.

Conceptual Overview

We explicitly preserve three gait features that are associated with perceived expressivity and transferable to the duck-like robot: head orientation, forward speed, and gait period.

Proposed Framework

A walking pattern generator and inverse kinematics create reference motions from the human gait cues. For each emotion, a separate reinforcement learning policy is trained on its reference set with the same reward function, enabling stable execution on the physical robot.
